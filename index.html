1. Executive Summary

This interactive dashboard presents the results of the conditioning sensitivity analysis for the FaciesGAN generative system. We assess the effect of incorporating local hard data (wells) and regional soft constraints (seismic reflection maps) on the generated geological structures, latent space manifold representation, and rock physics property consistency.

2. Conditioning Inputs (Seismic & Wells)

Seismic Conditioning Profiles

The regional seismic dataset consists of reflection amplitude profiles along the vertical xz plane, directing the spatial distribution of generated sand/shale channel geometries (plotted with the RdBu colormap):

Geophysical Context: Seismic reflections map acoustic impedance boundaries in the subsurface. Red and blue bands show positive and negative reflection coefficients, representing changes in velocity and density at layer boundaries. The generator uses this soft conditioning as a spatial guide, aligning its channel shapes with the geophysical trends.

Well Conditioning Traces

The well dataset contains local hard facies observations along vertical well traces (represented as color-coded lines on a white background):

Geological Context: Well logs act as 1D high-resolution 'hard data'. They capture the exact sequence of lithofacies (Floodplain, Channel, Point Bar, Boundary) encountered at specific coordinates. The GAN is penalized if its realizations do not match the facies observed at these well locations, ensuring local honoring of control wells.

3. Training Pyramid Architecture

The training dataset and model outputs are structured as multi-resolution pyramids. Use the tabs below to switch between the Ground Truth (Real) data used for training and the Generated realizations produced by each variant at all scales.

Why a Pyramid? Training GANs directly on high-resolution geological structures often causes mode collapse and training instability. By using a progressive multi-scale pyramid (Scale 0 to Scale 4), the network first learns large-scale patterns (river paths) and then refines details (point bars, channel edges, and fine noise) at finer scales.

6. Subsurface Uncertainty Analysis (1000 Realizations)

This section evaluates the local spatial uncertainty for each variant by generating 1000 realizations conformed to the same conditioning data profile (at training index 100). We compute the mismatch probability for categorical facies, and the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for acoustic impedance (Ip) and synthetic seismic relative to the ground truth:

Understanding Uncertainty Maps:
• Facies Mismatch Probability: Indicates how often the generated facies class at a pixel differs from the ground truth. Red indicators show well locations where conditional variants should have 0% uncertainty.
• Ip / Seismic Mean Absolute Error: Measures the average physical deviation from the ground truth. Lower values represent higher confidence in the prediction.

Unified Scorecard & Distribution Metrics Interpretation:
Evaluation Penalties:
• Facies RMSE & Phys Dev: Deviation of mean facies proportions and rock physics properties from real data.
• Ip WD Pen: Normalized Wasserstein distance penalty for acoustic impedance (Ip).
• Conn Dev: Absolute deviation of spatial connectivity (largest component fraction).
• Struct Div: Pairwise SSIM penalty (higher penalty = structural replication/mode collapse).
• Prop Var: Penalty for mismatch in facies proportion variance across realizations.
• Ip Spread: Penalty for insufficient variance in Ip across realizations (using 100% target std).
Distribution Distance Metrics:
• KL-Divergence (Kullback-Leibler): Information entropy mismatch (0 = identical distributions). High values indicate poor extreme-value sampling.
• Wasserstein Distance (WD): Work needed to morph generated property distribution to target. Lower is better.
• Color Indicators: Green represents excellent alignment (distance < 0.05), Yellow is acceptable (0.05 - 0.15), and Red indicates significant deviation (> 0.15).

Connectivity Metric Interpretation:
• Avg # Components: The average count of disconnected geobodies representing the channel facies (class 2). A lower component count indicates a more consolidated, continuous channel network. A higher count suggests fragmentation into smaller, disconnected mud/sand pockets.
• Largest Component (%): The percentage of the total channel area occupied by the single largest connected body. Higher values indicate stronger topological connectivity, representing that the model has successfully synthesized continuous flow paths rather than isolated pockets.
• Mean Component Size (px): The average size of individual connected bodies in pixels. If the generated mean size is larger than the real one, the model might be generating excessively thick or merged channels (over-connectivity). If it is smaller, the channels are too thin or fragmented.

9. Cross-Variant Embedding Synthesis

Manifold learning methods (MDS, UMAP, Isomap, and t-SNE) project high-dimensional geological models into a 2D space. This allows us to visualize the latent structure learned by the networks and evaluate model generalization:

Manifold Interpretation Guide:
• Real Data Cloud: Represented as the background distribution. It defines the 'true' geological variability space.
• Variant Overlap: Ideal realizations should overlap perfectly with the real data cloud. If a variant forms a tiny cluster far away, it represents a model that has suffered from mode collapse or significant bias.
• Dimensionality Reduction: t-SNE and UMAP focus on local structures (neighbor relationships), showing clusters of similar realizations, while MDS and Isomap preserve global distances and non-linear paths respectively.
📖 What are Latent Space Projections (Embeddings)?

Latent Space Projection is a technique used to visualize high-dimensional data by mapping it onto a 2D or 3D coordinate system. In the context of FaciesGAN, each generated geological model consists of thousands of pixels across multiple rock properties (Facies, Ip). To understand the diversity and quality of these models, we "project" them into a lower-dimensional **Latent Space**.

Models that share similar spatial features (e.g., similar channel orientation, connectivity, or rock physics correlations) will be positioned close to each other in this space, while geologically distinct models will be far apart. This allows us to visually confirm if our GAN is covering the full range of variability seen in the real reservoir data.

Key Manifold Learning Techniques:

1. MDS (Multidimensional Scaling):
A linear technique that attempts to preserve the actual Euclidean distances between models. It provides a reliable view of the "global" structure of the dataset.
2. Isomap:
A non-linear method that measures distances along the surface of the data manifold (geodesic distance). It is excellent for capturing continuous geological transitions, such as varying channel sinuosity.
3. t-SNE:
A probabilistic technique that prioritizes keeping similar models together. It is highly effective at revealing distinct clusters or "modes" in the data, making it useful for detecting mode collapse.
4. UMAP:
A state-of-the-art technique that balances local and global structure. It is often more efficient and provides a clearer separation of variants while still respecting the overall distribution of the real data.

11. Spatial Continuity Analysis

Variograms quantify the spatial correlation and continuity of geological properties by measuring how the variance between data points increases with distance (lag). This analysis evaluates whether the GAN-generated realizations (Facies, Ip, and Seismic) correctly capture the characteristic spatial structures of the original reservoir data.

Key variogram parameters used for evaluation include:

  • Sill: The maximum variance reached at large distances, indicating the total variability of the field.
  • Range: The distance at which the variogram reaches the sill, representing the maximum correlation length.
  • Nugget: The variance at zero lag, reflecting small-scale variability or measurement noise.

A close match between the 'Real' and 'Generated' variogram curves suggests that the GAN has successfully learned the underlying geological length scales and spatial textures:

Variogram Interpretation Guide:
• Curvature Match: A variant curve that follows the real curve indicates correct spatial autocorrelation. If the curve rises too fast, the generated features are too noisy or disconnected at small scales.
• Sill Level (Total Variance): If the variant's sill is lower than the real sill, it means the generated realizations lack variance (i.e. they are too homogeneous/blurry).
• Range (Correlation Length): A mismatch in the range means the generated channels are either too long/continuous or too short/fragmented compared to reality.
